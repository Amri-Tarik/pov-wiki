Combat and building¶

Critical strikes, evasion, resistances, what creatures pierce, and building costs.

Numbers are the defaults of the Normal difficulty; servers can change them. Every formula here is checked against the mod's code with each patch.

Each point of an attribute gives:

+0.2 carry weight		 12.5% increased health regeneration

Weapons governed by attributes multiply their damage by a random skill multiplier, picked anew for every hit between its midpoint − 0.15 and + 0.15, never below 0. Order of a hit¶

Every hit of yours, weapon or spell, goes through these steps in this order. Increases all go into one sum wherever they come from, so their place changes nothing; the places of flat damage and multipliers do matter.

When you make the hit

  1. Valheim's damage of the weapon at its quality, × the attack's own multiplier (a combo's last hit and the like) × your skill multiplier.
  2. Weapon oils add their damage.
  3. Flat damage, × the skill multiplier.
  4. Conversion, then damage gained as extra; slams and aftershocks deal what is left of slash and pierce as blunt at 50%.
  5. Increases: × (1 + their sum), each damage type with its own.
  6. Bows and crossbows: the ammo's share of the damage swaps your bow or crossbow damage increases for projectile damage increases.
  7. More multipliers, one after another, rage last.
  8. More projectiles damage.

When it reaches the enemy

  1. Critical strike damage: × its multiplier, on the whole hit.
  2. Bows and crossbows: effective range, × its factor.
  3. Increases against the enemy join the sum; then flat damage against the enemy, which the crit doesn't multiply.
  4. Increases and more multipliers that need the hit and the enemy together.
  5. Armour break adds to the hit's physical damage, and impale multiplies it.
  6. Valheim applies the creature's resistances, with your penetration for fire, frost, lightning and poison.

So flat damage grows with every increase and multiplier after it, increases only add to each other, and a critical strike multiplies everything except flat damage against the enemy.

Hit damage¶

A hit's damage is built in the parts below; Order of a hit lists them in the order they happen.

Flat damage¶

Flat damage is added before any increase, × your skill multiplier (the same one the weapon's own damage gets). Where it goes:

  • general flat damage, and flat damage for the kind of hit (your weapon kind, melee, spells, projectiles, attacks, alternative attacks and the like): to the hit's largest damage type
  • flat physical damage: to the largest of slash, pierce and blunt (blunt if the hit has none)
  • flat slash, pierce or blunt damage: only to a hit that already has that type
  • flat fire, frost, lightning, poison or spirit damage: always, as that type
  • flat elemental damage: to the largest of the hit's fire, frost and lightning, only if it has one of them

Weapon oils add their damage type before flat damage, without the skill multiplier.

A hit with no damage at all gets neither oils nor flat damage.

Conversion¶

Damage conversion turns part of a hit's slash, pierce and blunt damage into another type, after flat damage and before increases; no other type converts. Each of the three converts by its own conversions plus those of all physical damage; if they come to more than 100%, they're scaled down to 100% together.

Conversion that depends on the enemy (against bosses, enemies with ailments and the like) counts too.

converted = slash, pierce or blunt × its conversion to the new type, all its conversions together at most 100%

Converted damage is its new type for resistances and ailments, but it grows with the increases of both types: its old type's and its new type's, added together.

Damage gained as extra: worked out from the slash, pierce and blunt damage before conversion, and added after it as its new type. It grows with its new type's increases only.

Increases¶

All the increased and reduced damage that applies to a hit is added into one sum, and the hit is multiplied by 1 + that sum once: general damage, your weapon kind's, melee, ranged or spell damage, attacks', alternative attacks', that of hits that aren't projectiles, a running attack's bonus, and the increases that depend on the enemy (below).

A scepter's hits count as the kind of attack they are: its spells take spell damage increases, its other attacks melee or ranged ones.

A sceptre's Smite also takes your area damage increases and flat area damage.

damage of a type = (weapon damage × skill multiplier + oils + flat damage × skill multiplier) × (1 + increases + that type's increases)

Each damage type adds its own increases to the sum, for that type only: fire, frost and lightning also add elemental damage increases, and slash, pierce and blunt add physical damage increases. So +50% damage and +50% fire damage make fire damage × 2, not × 2.25.

Projectiles: projectile damage increases join the sum too, with the projectile speed increases any of your stats turn into damage, and so do a torch's damage increases.

Bows and crossbows split each shot in two parts. The ammo's share is its damage ÷ (the weapon's damage + the ammo's). That part takes the sum without your bow or crossbow damage increases, plus your projectile damage increases (bow or crossbow projectile ones included); the weapon's part takes the sum as it is, without projectile increases.

damage = weapon's part × (1 + sum) + ammo's part × (1 + sum − bow or crossbow increases + projectile increases)

Chopping and mining: increases against trees, ore and resources join the same sum, and so flat damage against them grows with the whole sum.

More multipliers¶

More (and less) damage multiplies the hit separately, each multiplier after the other: more damage and more damage for your weapon kind, then rage: × (1 + 1% per point × (1 + rage effect increases)), not on spells.

More (and less) projectile damage then multiplies projectiles on its own.

Against the enemy¶

When the hit reaches an enemy, the increases that depend on it join the same sum:

  • increases against enemies of a kind or in a state (bosses, enemies with ailments and the like)
  • backstab: against an enemy that hasn't noticed you; Valheim's own backstab bonus is turned off
  • +15% while the enemy is staggered, instead of Valheim's double damage (stagger)
  • charged attack: +25%

Flat damage against the enemy (flat backstab damage, flat damage against enemies of a kind) goes to the hit's largest type, × your skill multiplier × (1 + the whole sum). It comes after the critical hit, so a crit doesn't multiply it.

Last come the increases and more multipliers that need something of the hit and something of the enemy together; the increases join the same sum.

Effective range¶

Effective Range: a bow or crossbow shot deals full damage up to a start distance from where it was shot, then less the further it flies: × 0.8 at 37.5% of the way to an end distance, and × 0.2 at the end distance and beyond. Bows: 12 m, 18 m and 28 m; crossbows: 12 m, 18 m and 28 m. Thrown weapons and spells don't fall off.

damage factor: × 1 at start, falling in a straight line to × 0.8 at start + 37.5% × (end − start), then in a straight line to × 0.2 at end; start and end × range

Range is 1 + your shooting effective range increases (bow or crossbow ones count for that weapon), so +50% puts both distances 1.5 times as far. The factor multiplies the hit when it reaches the enemy, after the critical strike; flat damage against the enemy is added after it and isn't reduced.

For scale: a Wood Floor 2x2 is 2 m on a side, so a bow's 12 m and 28 m are 6 and 14 floors in a row, a crossbow's 12 m and 28 m 6 and 14.

Crossbow magazine¶

Crossbows shoot from a magazine. Each crossbow reload (Valheim's reload, shortened by reload speed) fills it:

[loaded bolts](../glossary/loaded-bolts.md) = ⌊(1 + flat loaded bolts + additional projectiles + ⌊additional projectile chance⌋, +1 with the chance of what is left) × (1 + loaded bolt increases) × more⌋, rolled when the reload finishes
time between shots = the crossbow's reload time × 0.2857 ÷ ((1 + [crossbow fire rate](../glossary/crossbow-fire-rate.md) increases) × more)

Each shot fires one bolt and uses one loaded shot; a shot that saves its ammo keeps it. When the magazine is empty, the crossbow reloads; the Reload key (R by default) reloads early whenever the magazine isn't full. The first shot after a reload and the last loaded shot are magazine shots: bonuses can name them.

Critical damage increases for the last loaded shot change how its critical strike multiplier is built.

Your penetration¶

Valheim gives each creature a step of resistance against each damage type. Your resistance penetration against fire, frost, lightning and poison (elemental penetration counts for the first three) adds to the share of the damage that gets through:

damage dealt = damage × max(0, 1 − resistance + penetration)
Creature's step Resistance Damage dealt With 10% penetration With 25% penetration With 50% penetration
Very weak -100% 200% 210% 225% 250%
Weak -50% 150% 160% 175% 200%
Slightly weak -25% 125% 135% 150% 175%
Normal 0% 100% 110% 125% 150%
Slightly resistant 25% 75% 85% 100% 125%
Resistant 50% 50% 60% 75% 100%
Very resistant 75% 25% 35% 50% 75%
Immune 100% 0% 10% 25% 50%

Immunity counts as 100% resistance, so penetration lets its own share through an immunity. Against a weakness it adds on top. Physical and spirit damage have no penetration.

Critical strike damage¶

Critical hits multiply the hit's damage by:

1.5 + critical damage bonuses, at least 0

The bonuses come in parts, each its flat bonuses × (1 + increases): general ones, those for your weapon kind, for the target, and for the alternative attack. The parts are computed separately and added up, so a general "reduced critical damage" doesn't lower your weapon's own bonus.

On a crossbow's last loaded shot (magazine), if you have critical damage increases for that shot, the multiplier is 1 + (0.5 + flat critical damage bonuses) × (1 + every critical damage increase that applies to the shot) × more critical damage: the increases then multiply the base too.

Critical strike chance¶

Critical strike chance starts from the weapon's base:

chance = (base + flat) × (1 + increases) × more, at most the maximum

Base: knives 15%, polearms 10%, other weapons 5%, spells 5%. The weapon's own critical chance properties apply to its base first. The maximum is 100% by default.

Evasion¶

Evasion rating gives a chance to avoid a hit, which depends on the attacker's accuracy (enemy accuracy), growing with its level and stars:

accuracy = (30 + 0.48 × level) × (1 + 15% × stars)
evade chance = evasion ÷ (3 × accuracy), at most 150%

Every evasion restarts your stamina regeneration delay, even one that costs no stamina; with Unbroken Footing, evasions leave stamina regeneration running.

An evaded hit applies nothing: no damage, ailments, status effects, stagger or knockback; nor does a hit you dodge.

An evasion costs stamina by how much of the hit would reach your health after all your defences (armour, resistances, glancing, rune shield, endurance): 4% of your maximum stamina for 30% of your maximum health or less, rising evenly to 18% at 2× your maximum health and beyond. Cost reductions for evading lower it, and a chance to evade without stamina makes it free; with less stamina than it costs, you can't evade.

Each hit is rolled on its own. Every hit you evade lowers your chance against every attacker: × 80% per evasion, until 8 s pass without a hit you could evade. A chance above 100% evades for sure until your evasions bring it under. Hits with no attacker (a spell whose caster is gone, say) are rolled against a level-1 creature's accuracy. Damage over time can't be evaded. Chance against creatures without stars, before any evasion, by biome:

Resistances¶

Resistances reduce damage of their type: damage × (1 − resistance).

resistance = min(cap, your resistance − enemy penetration); cap = 75% + maximum resistance, at most 90%

Fire, frost and lightning also count elemental resistance. Penetration can take resistance below zero, which increases the damage taken.

Armour¶

Armour is Valheim's own: it reduces each hit by the same share for all its damage types, much against small hits, less against big ones. With hit damage the sum of the hit's damage types:

armour < damage ÷ 2: damage taken = damage − armour; otherwise: damage taken = damage² ÷ (4 × armour)
armour against creatures = your armour × 1.05 × (1 + armour effectiveness increases) − the creature's armour penetration

Armour applies after rune shield and block. Creatures ignore part of it: see enemy penetration. Damage taken, by armour and hit:

Armour Hit of 20 Hit of 50 Hit of 100 Hit of 200 Hit of 500

Rune shield¶

Rune shield is a layer over your health. Of a hit that gets through block, resistances and armour, it takes everything but spirit and poison damage until it runs out; the rest goes to your health. While it holds, stagger you take is cut by 50%.

maximum = (base + flat + armour's rune shield + food + from health) × (1 + increases)

Valheim's shield effects (such as the Staff of Protection's) become a ward on whoever gets them: it takes Valheim's amount of damage, everything but spirit and poison, for Valheim's duration (on you, longer with buff duration, at most × 3). While a ward holds, stagger taken is cut by 50% too.

The base is 0 by default (a server setting): only the tree, gear and food give a shield. Stats that turn health into shield give maximum health × p ÷ (1 − p) for a share p, at most 90%.

Damage you take restarts the wait. After 5 s without damage, it recharges 20% of its maximum per second (5 s from empty), faster with shield regeneration. Recharge delay reductions cut the wait by at most 75%, never below 0.25 s.

Enemy penetration¶

Creatures pierce your defences by their biome: with flat armour penetration and resistance penetration (a share of your resistances). Your armour counts for 105% against creatures' hits, more with armour effectiveness increases.

Biome Armour ignored Resistance ignored
Meadows 0 0%
Black Forest 8 0%
Swamp 15 0%
Mountain 25 5%
Plains 35 10%
Mistlands 50 15%
Ashlands 70 20%
Deep North 90 25%

Bosses move toward the next biome's values. Creatures with the Piercing modifier ignore more.

Stagger¶

Stagger fills from a hit's blunt, slash, pierce and lightning damage, as the target takes it. At its threshold, maximum health × the creature's own stagger factor, the target staggers. The gauge then drains: from full in 5 s for players, 25 s for enemies (the mod slows it).

Your own threshold counts 100% of your maximum rune shield as health, and your gauge drains that much faster, so it still empties in the same time.

your hit's stagger = (damage × 20% + flat) × (1 + stagger build-up) × the weapon's stagger multiplier

Your hits carry 20% of the stagger power Valheim gives them (a server setting); stagger build-up multiplies it and flat stagger build-up adds to it.

Forced stagger: when it rolls, the hit's stagger is multiplied by 100.

Stagger you take: × (1 − stagger resistance), which is at most 85%; cut by 50% while your rune shield holds. Stagger recovery drains your gauge faster.

Block¶

Block: when you block a hit from the front, Valheim treats your block power like armour against the whole hit, every damage type, after the blocking item's own resistances.

block power = max(0, Valheim's block power × (1 + the shield's own block power increases) + flat block power) × (1 + block power increases)
Valheim's block power = the item's block power at its quality × (1 + Blocking skill × 0.5)
parry: block power × the item's parry bonus × (1 + parry bonus increases)

A parry is a block within 0.25 s of raising your guard, with an item that has a parry bonus. A block costs Valheim's block stamina × the share of your block power the hit used; a parry costs a fixed amount.

What gets through works like armour: block power under half the hit takes that much off; more than that cuts the hit to hit² ÷ (4 × block power). The rest goes on to your armour and rune shield.

If you're out of stamina, or the blocked hit's stagger breaks your guard, nothing is blocked. A hit that meets your block can't be a Glancing Blow.

Glancing blows¶

Glancing Blow: any hit a creature lands on you that doesn't meet your block can glance. Everyone has 25% chance (a server setting), plus glancing blow chance.

glancing blow: damage taken − 35% × (1 + glancing blow reduction increases), that cut kept between 5% and 90%

The cut joins your other damage-taken modifiers, which together can take off at most 60%. It comes before your resistances, block and armour.

Hits that meet your block don't glance; with Edge of the Block, a blocked hit that breaks your guard still can.

Endurance¶

Endurance: while your health is under your endurance threshold, the part of any damage you take below the threshold is reduced, hits and damage over time alike.

damage taken = damage − (the part of it below the threshold) × endurance reduction

Reduction is 20% at base, × (1 + increases), at most 75%. The threshold is in health points; with Rune Line it's compared to your rune shield instead.

It applies last, after your resistances, block and armour, just before your rune shield takes the damage.

Thorns¶

Thorns: when a creature hits you and you don't evade it, it takes your thorns damage back. The amount doesn't depend on the hit: it's your flat thorns, or 5 if increased thorns are all you have.

thorns damage = flat thorns × (1 + damage increases + thorns increases + the damage type's increases) × block or parry multiplier × distance

It's pierce damage, unless your stats convert part of it to other types or add more as extra damage.

Full damage to an attacker within 3 m of you (from its edge), falling to nothing at 8 m.

On a shield block with Spiked Shield: × 150%. Thorns that work with parries add to a parry's thorns.

Thorny Carapace also deals your thorns to enemies touching you, once every 1 s per enemy, shorter with cooldown reduction and recovery.

Charged attacks¶

Charged attack: something charges your next weapon attack, and the next melee or ranged attack you release spends it, hit or miss (spells only with ChargeApplicability). One charge waits at a time; it doesn't stack or expire.

charged attack: + 25% increased damage, added to your other damage increases, and rows that work with charged attacks apply

A source charges again only 2 s after it last charged the attack (critical hits: 0.2 s). After any charge, every source also waits the shorter of its own cooldown and 3 s (cut by cooldown reduction and recovery). A perfect dodge ignores both cooldowns, but can't charge an attack that's already charged.

What charges the attack. Each source needs the passive or mastery option that grants it:

Charges the attack when Granted by
an alternative attack hits an enemy Second edge
you activate a trinket Flash Response
you cast a spell Spell Primes the Strike
you cover 25 m running on the ground or levitating, while no charge waits Giant's Run-Up
you jump Leap Before the Blow
you land a critical hit with an attack Critical Charge
you parry a hit Ready Answer
you start a dodge roll (a perfect dodge charges it even on cooldown) Rolling Charge

With Choose the Spend, only alternative attacks spend the charge; with Alternate Leads, alternative attacks don't spend it.

Unbound Charge: rolled once when the charge is spent. On success the attack gets 50% increased damage instead, and every row that works with charged attacks counts twice.

Hit splash: a charged attack's first hit on an enemy also hits every other enemy within 3 m × (1 + area of effect increases), for your splash share of its damage.

Additional strikes¶

Strike: a melee weapon's vertical swing, primary or alternative (horizontal swings and thrusts don't count, whatever their animation); slams and sceptres don't count. Once a swing hits an enemy, it hits again as many times as your additional strikes, one every 0.12 s. Each goes to the nearest enemy not struck yet, within 5 m of the first target and 4.5 m of you; when there is none left, they stop.

additional strikes = ⌊additional strikes⌋, ×2 once after a critical strike (Double on Crit)

Each additional strike is a new hit of the same attack: it rolls its own critical strike and gets all your damage bonuses. Strike bonuses apply to the swing and to every additional strike.

With Double on Crit, the attack's first critical strike, from the swing or from one of its strikes, doubles its additional strikes, once per attack.

With All on One Target, every additional strike hits the first target instead, at 50% damage each, and they stop if it dies or gets out of reach.

Combo counts one hit per attack: additional strikes and phantom strikes never add to it.

A sceptre's strike is its Smite: it takes additional strikes as additional Smites.

Primary attacks that are strikes: Antler Pickaxe, Black Metal Pickaxe, Bronze Pickaxe, Iron Pickaxe, Stone Pickaxe.

Alternative attacks that are strikes: Black Metal Axe, Bloodgeon, Bronze Axe, Bronze Mace, Flametal Mace, Flint Axe, Frostfire Axe, Frostfire Mace, Frostner, Iron Axe, Iron Mace, Jotun Bane, Klossen, Nord Axe, Nord Mace, Porcupine, Stone Axe, Storm Star, Thunderblood Axe, Thunderblood Mace, Wooden Axe, Wooden Mace.

Smite¶

Every swing of the Sceptre of the Pious calls down Smite, a bolt of lightning. The swing itself hits like a club, with only the sceptre's physical damage; Smite carries its elemental damage (fire, frost, lightning, poison and spirit) at the sceptre's quality, and each swing costs 5 eitr. It lands on the first enemy the swing hit; if the swing hit none, on the nearest enemy within its radius of a point 2.5 m in front of you, or else on that point.

It hits every creature within 2.5 m × your area of effect of where it lands, except players and tamed creatures, × the sceptre's skill multiplier. Its hits can't be blocked or dodged. Each of them is a spell hit, a strike and an area hit at once (spells, strike): spell, strike and area damage bonuses all apply.

Additional Smites: your strike targets plus your additional strikes, whole ones only. Each lands on a different random enemy within 8 m × your area increases of the first, never on the first enemy again, and hits in the same way; with fewer enemies around, fewer land.

With All on One Target, they all strike the first enemy instead, at 50% damage each, and stop if it dies; with no enemy to land on, there are none.

With Double on Crit, a critical strike from one of a cast's Smites doubles its additional Smites, once per cast.

Casting Smite ends your combo.

Ground Effects: every Smite also leaves a spell ground patch where it lands.

A thrown Sceptre of the Pious casts Smite where it lands, with the throw's damage.

Phantom strikes¶

Phantom Strikes can follow a melee attack hit: the weapon's own blow or one of its additional strikes, not projectiles, area blows or other copies. Each hits a random enemy within 6 m of the struck one, that one included: the first 0.15 s after the hit, then one every 0.1 s.

phantom strikes per hit = ⌊chance⌋, +1 with a probability of what is left
phantom strike damage = 24% × the hit's damage without its critical strike

The hit's damage is taken with all your damage bonuses applied but its critical hit taken out, before the enemy's armour and resistances. A phantom strike doesn't get your bonuses again, but it rolls a critical strike of its own: your weapon's crit chance against the enemy it hits (with rows for phantom strikes), and your weapon's crit multiplier. The enemy's armour and resistances apply to it as usual.

Stagger: a phantom strike carries 50% of the hit's stagger, with the stagger build-up that works with phantom strikes in place of the hit's own.

Ailment gauge: phantom strikes don't fill it. Their fire, poison and spirit damage hits at once instead of over time, and their frost and lightning bring no chill or shock.

With Endless Apparition, a phantom strike that lands can cause more: the n-th generation's chance is the first's × the factor its line gives, to the power n − 1. At most 96 phantom strikes wait at once.

Area of effect¶

Area of effect widens attacks in a straight line: the factor multiplies the hit radius, not the ground it covers. +100% area doubles the radius and covers four times the ground.

radius × max(0.1, (1 + area increases) × area multipliers)

Area attacks (a slam is one) get the wider radius; other swings reach further as well.

Slams and aftershocks¶

Slam: the sledge's area swing counts (Valheim's other area attacks don't), and so does an alternative attack Ground Strike turns into one: it uses the weapon's own area radius, or 3 m if the weapon has no area attack. Your area of effect widens a slam's radius.

A slam's slash and pierce damage, after their own conversions, is dealt as blunt at 50%; the rest of it is lost. Its blunt, elemental and other damage stay whole, and its blunt (with what slash and pierce added to it) converts as usual. Slash and pierce gained as extra fold into blunt the same way. Flat damage that applies to slams or area hits but not to the weapon's other hits is added to it as blunt.

Aftershock: rolled once per slam, at most 100%. 0.45 s later it erupts at the slam's point, in the slam's radius × the area increases that only apply to aftershocks, and hits everything there with the slam's full damage. An aftershock never rolls another, though repeat chance can make it erupt once more.

An aftershock pushes enemies away from where it erupts.

A thrown club, mace or sledge that lands on the ground (not on a creature) slams there once: 3 m × your area of effect, with 100% of the weapon's damage × your skill multiplier, against every enemy and object in it. A thrown sceptre that lands casts its spell there instead.

A charged attack that isn't a slam can make aftershocks too, from aftershock chance that works with charged attacks: each full 100% gives one, and the rest is a chance for one more, each 0.45 s after the last, where the enemy was hit.

Primary attacks that are slams: Demolisher, Frostfire Sledge, Iron Sledge, Nord Sledge, Stagbreaker, Thunderblood Sledge, Wooden Sledge.

Alternative attacks that Ground Strike turns into slams: Battleaxe, Berserkir Axes, Black Metal Atgeir, Black Metal Battleaxe, Bleeding Berserkir Axes, Bronze Atgeir, Brutal Slayer, Crystal Battleaxe, Demolisher, Early Axes, Frostfire Atgeir, Frostfire Greataxe, Frostfire Greatsword, Frostfire Sledge, Himminafl, Iron Atgeir, Iron Sledge, Krom, Nord Atgeir, Nord Greataxe, Nord Greatsword, Nord Sledge, Primal Berserkir Axes, Primal Slayer, Scourging Slayer, Skull Splittur, Slayer, Stagbreaker, Thunderblood Atgeir, Thunderblood Greataxe, Thunderblood Greatsword, Thunderblood Sledge, Thundering Berserkir Axes, Wooden Atgeir, Wooden Battleaxe, Wooden Greatsword, Wooden Sledge.

Ailments¶

Ailment gauge: bleeding, burning, poison, chill and shock each fill their own on the enemy, from the matching part of your hits' damage as the enemy takes it, after its resistances and armour: slash, fire, poison, frost and lightning.

gauge filled by a hit = matching damage ÷ (62.5% × the enemy's base health) × (1 + build-up)

How much of the gauge one hit fills, by its damage as a share of the enemy's base health (its maximum health without the mod's difficulty scaling):

Damage ÷ base health 5% 10% 25% 50%
Filled, +0% build-up 8% 16% 40% 80%
Filled, +100% build-up 16% 32% 80% 100%

At 100% the ailment starts, from the strongest matching hit since the gauge began to fill. The gauge then stays full, so every further matching hit applies the ailment again from its own damage. 3 s after the last matching hit the gauge drains, 25% per second. Chill ends when it is empty (at most 7 s after that hit); the other ailments run their own time.

What each ailment does with that damage:

  • Bleeding: 20% of the slash damage per second for 4 s (80% in all), × your ailment damage multipliers and damage over time multiplier (physical). Longer duration adds seconds, so more damage. One bleed per player on an enemy: a new one replaces yours only if it deals at least as much per second.
  • Burning: 25% of the fire damage per second for 4 s (100% in all), × your ailment damage multipliers and damage over time multiplier (fire); fire damage increases don't apply again. Longer duration adds seconds, so more damage. One burn per player on an enemy: a new one replaces yours only if it deals at least as much per second. A wet enemy burns out 6 times as fast.
  • Poison: 15% of the poison damage per second for 4 s (60% in all), × your ailment damage multipliers and damage over time multiplier (poison), plus any share of the enemy's base health your stats add, spread over the same 4 s (ailment damage doesn't raise that part). Longer duration adds seconds. Each player can keep 3 poisons on an enemy (more with limit bonuses); past that, a new one replaces your weakest if it would deal more. Skeletons and the Charred take poison damage normally, but their poison gauge fills at half speed; summoned skeletons stay immune.
  • Chill: Valheim's frost effect, its strength × (1 + your general and chill ailment damage increases); ailment and status duration make it last longer.

  • Shock: for 3 s the enemy takes more damage from every source and builds up stagger faster, by the shock's strength. Only the strongest shock on an enemy acts, whoever applied it. Flat ailment damage does nothing for shock.

    shock strength = 0.5 × (lightning damage ÷ (62.5% × the enemy's base health))^0.4 × (1 + shock effect increases) × more, at most 50%; under 5%, no shock

    The lightning damage here is the hit's, before the enemy's resistances and armour.

Ailment damage multipliers for burning, poison and bleeding: × (1 + ailment damage increases, general and that ailment's) × their more multipliers; flat ailment damage adds its amount spread over 4 s. Each player's ailments tick on their own, once a second.

With Deferred Doom, only critical strikes inflict ailments, and the damage over time of the ailments they inflict is × your critical strike multiplier.

Armour break¶

Armour break: a hit's blunt damage breaks the enemy's armour the way it would fill an ailment gauge: blunt against its base health, raised by build-up. The broken share adds up to 100% and is shared by everyone hitting the enemy; 8 s after the last hit that added to it, it's gone.

broken share added by a hit = blunt ÷ (ailment threshold × the enemy's base health) × (1 + build-up)
your physical damage + the enemy's armour × broken share × (1 + armour break effect increases), that share at most 100%

Every hit of yours on an enemy with broken armour gets that added, including the hit that just added to it, spread over its blunt, slash and pierce (as blunt if it has none). It comes before the enemy's armour, so it pays back about as much as the armour takes.

Impale¶

Impale: a hit's pierce damage can impale the enemy. The chance works like the ailment gauge: pierce against the enemy's base health, raised by build-up.

chance = (pierce ÷ (ailment threshold × the enemy's base health) + flat build-up) × (1 + build-up increases), at most 100%
physical damage your hits deal × (1 + impales × 5% × (1 + ailment damage increases))

An enemy holds up to 5 impales (more with impale limit). Each new impale refreshes them all for 8 s × (1 + ailment duration increases), at least 0.5 s.

Limits¶

Limit: a bonus that names an effect raises only that effect. One that names none raises every limit below at once. Bonuses add up first, and the total is rounded down (but not for the infusion store).

Limit Base
Thralls per cast of a summoning staff 1
Spores at once 3
Ice crystals at once 3
Armour shards at once 3
Impales one enemy can hold 5
Links you can hold at once 1
Ground Effects: patches at once 1
Infusion: units stored 10

Chain Lightning's targets (3) are the exception: they only count limit bonuses that name Chain Lightning.

Ground effects¶

Ground Effects: a passive that creates ground for a damage type (Ashen Wake, Blighted Soil, Glassbound, Stormbed) makes your spells leave a patch where they land: a projectile that hits the ground or an object (not one that hits an enemy), or an area spell where it strikes. One impact makes one patch, with a layer for each of the spell's fire, frost, lightning and poison damage that has such a passive.

Each layer comes from the spell's damage of its type, after your damage increases (ground damage increases join them) and before its critical strike:

damage per second = 20% × that damage × your damage over time multiplier for the type
build-up per second = 20% × that damage, into burning, chill, shock or poison

A patch has a radius of 2 m × (1 + area increases; a spell's patch also takes spell area increases), at most 12 m, and lasts 4 s × (1 + effect duration increases), at most 60 s.

Enemies standing on it (on the ground, not swimming) take its damage every 0.2 s, paid about once a second, through their resistances. Ground damage is never a critical strike, can't be blocked or evaded, and doesn't stagger. Each enemy takes only the strongest patch of each type, whoever made it; different types add up. Your allies and you take no damage from your patches.

You can keep 1 patch at once (limits); a new one past the limit removes your oldest. Only spell patches count.

Chilled Ground makes everyone on it slide, you and your allies too. It doesn't lower speed: speeding up and braking work at 15% of the usual rate, turning at 35%.

Passives that work while you stand on a kind of ground count any patch of that type, yours or not.

Infusion¶

Infusion: with Gather the Elements and a staff in hand (elemental or blood magic), standing on your own spell patches absorbs them into a store, by damage type. A patch shrinks as it's absorbed and is gone below 0.3 m.

units gained = 6 m² per second × (1 + absorption rate) ÷ 2 m²

Less is gained where your other patches of the same type already cover the patch's edge.

The store holds at most 10 units in all (limits). It empties 10 s after the last gain, and when you die.

Your next damaging spell cast by hand (or attack with Sceptre of the Pious) uses the whole store: each unit adds 6% of the damage of the spell that made the patch (that type, after its increases) × (1 + infusion potency), as flat damage of its type. A projectile spell splits it among its projectiles; an area spell adds all of it to every hit. Damage that came from infusion can't be absorbed again.

Spell Echo repeats infused projectile spells; infused area spells aren't repeated.

Life leech¶

Life leech: a share of the health your weapon and projectile hits actually remove from an enemy; spells use spell leech. Bleeding, thorns and echoed hits don't leech.

leeched = health removed × life leech

Each hit starts its own leech, which gives back 2% of your maximum health per second until it's paid out. All of them together give at most 20% per second, which 10 leeches running at once reach. Reaching full health ends every leech still running.

Build cost¶

Build cost reductions lower each material of a piece, the mod's buildings included, by at most 50%:

cost = ⌈amount × (1 − reduction)⌉, reduction at most 50%

Dismantling gives back only what was paid. When a refund saves the materials of a placed piece, dismantling it gives nothing.
